=== 16 spreadsheet ===
My wife makes me log every purchase over $40, and then I found her secret $890 monthly charge. Am I wrong?

I'm 31, and my wife Whitney is 33. We've been married five years. We both work full time. I'm an electrician, and she does payroll for a dental group.

Three years ago, I bought a $300 guitar pedal without telling her. That was the big crime. Whitney cried for two days and told her mom I was "reckless with the family's future." After that, she made a rule.

Every purchase over $40 has to go in her spreadsheet. Date, amount, reason. And anything over $40 needs her approval before I buy it. She checks my card every Sunday night at the kitchen table, line by line, with a yellow highlighter.

I went along with it. I figured if it made her feel safe, fine. I'm not a big spender anyway.

Then last month my work boots split open on a job site. I bought a new pair on my lunch break. $62. I logged them that night like I was supposed to.

Whitney didn't yell. She went very quiet, and then her eyes filled up, and she said, "You know how hard I work to keep us afloat, and you just do whatever you want."

I said they were boots. For work. My old ones had a hole in the toe.

She said, "That's what you always say. There's always a reason."

So I asked a simple question. I asked if I could see her side of the spreadsheet. Her card, her purchases. Just to be fair.

She looked at me like I'd slapped her. She said, "Wow. So now you're monitoring me? That's really controlling, Ben."

Her mom called me the next day to tell me I was making Whitney feel unsafe in her own marriage.

So I dropped it. For about a week.

Then I was paying the electric bill online and noticed our savings balance was $4,200 lower than it should have been. And there was a charge I'd never seen. $890. Every month. For two years.

Would you have said something right away, or quietly started digging? Follow for part 2.
--- part ---
Part 2 of my wife making me log every $40 purchase while hiding an $890 monthly charge.

The charge said WL Holdings. That's it. No website, no phone number on the statement.

My first thought was the obvious one. A secret apartment. Somebody else. Two years of Sundays with a yellow highlighter, and the whole time she was paying rent somewhere I didn't know about.

I didn't say anything. I wanted to be sure before I blew up my marriage over three words on a bank statement.

I looked up WL Holdings. It's a storage company. Climate controlled units off the highway, about twenty minutes from our house.

So now I'm thinking, okay, maybe not an apartment. Maybe she's hiding stuff from an ex. Maybe she's planning to leave me and moving things out slowly. I honestly didn't know which would be worse.

That Saturday, Whitney said she was going to her sister's for brunch. I waited ten minutes and drove to the storage place.

Her car was in the lot.

I parked across the street and watched her walk out of the building carrying two shopping bags from a department store. She put them in her trunk, sat in her car for a minute, and then drove off toward her sister's like nothing happened.

When I got home, I went through the drawer where she keeps the important stuff. Passports, birth certificates, car titles. Taped to the bottom of the drawer was a little silver key with a tag on it. Unit 214.

I didn't take it. I just took a picture of it.

That night at dinner, Whitney asked if I'd logged the gas I bought on Friday. $43.

I said yes. She said, "Thank you. See how easy that is when you just follow the system?"

I smiled and said, "Yeah. Really easy."

On Monday I called in sick. I took the key out of the drawer, drove to the storage place, and found unit 214.

Part 3. I opened my wife's secret storage unit, the one she was paying for while making me log every $40 purchase.

The door rolled up, and I just stood there.

It wasn't another man's stuff. It wasn't her packing to leave me. It was shopping. Two years of shopping.

Handbags still in dust bags. Shoe boxes stacked to the ceiling. Three winter coats with the tags on. An espresso machine still in the box. A stand mixer in a color called pistachio. Rows of skincare sets. A rack of dresses.

On a little folding table in the back was a binder. I opened it, and my stomach dropped.

It was a spreadsheet. Printed out. Her spreadsheet, the same format she makes me use. Date, amount, reason. Except her reasons said things like "deserved it" and "rough week" and "sale."

The total at the bottom of the last page was $21,860.

Taped inside the front cover was a sticky note in her mom's handwriting. It said, "Keep him busy with his own receipts and he'll never look at yours."

I took a picture of every page. Then I locked the unit, put the key back under the drawer exactly where it was, and went home.

That Sunday night, she sat down with her highlighter like always. I'd logged a $48 drill bit set. She sighed and said, "Ben, we talked about this. We're trying to save for a house."

I said, "Are we?"

She said, "What is that supposed to mean?"

I said, "Nothing. I'll be more careful."

She actually patted my hand.

I called her sister the next morning, because I needed to know if everyone knew. Her sister went quiet, then said, "Ben, I didn't think it was my business. But Mom's been doing the same thing to Dad for thirty years."

Whitney's birthday dinner was in two weeks. Her parents were hosting. She had already told everyone I was bringing the cake.

I said I'd bring something better.

Should I have confronted her privately, or was the birthday dinner fair game? Follow for the final part.
--- part ---
Final part. I brought my wife's secret shopping spreadsheet to her birthday dinner, the same week she scolded me over a $48 drill set.

Her parents' dining room. Twelve people. Her dad at the head of the table, her mom right next to Whitney.

I brought the cake. I also brought a gift bag. Whitney opened it at the table, smiling, because everyone was watching.

Inside was the binder from unit 214. On the cover, I'd put a label that said, "Every purchase over $40. No exceptions."

She opened it, saw the first page, and closed it so fast the table shook.

Her mom said, "What is that?"

I said, "It's Whitney's system. She taught it to me. I just figured her side deserved the same respect."

Then I read her own text out loud. I'd saved it from three years ago. "Anything over $40 gets discussed. No exceptions. That's what partners do."

Whitney started crying. Not quiet crying, the real kind. She said I was humiliating her, that this was abuse, that I'd been stalking her.

Her dad hadn't said a word. He reached over, took the binder, and flipped to the inside cover. He read the sticky note. He looked at his wife for a long time.

Then he said, very calmly, "Carol, is this your handwriting?"

That was the end of the birthday dinner.

Whitney and I are separated now. She sold most of what was in the unit to pay back the savings account, and our lawyer made sure every dollar went where it belonged. I don't log anything anymore. I don't need anyone's permission to buy boots.

The funny part is her dad. He called me a week later and asked if I could help him go through thirty years of bank statements. He said, "I think I need a spreadsheet of my own."

We meet every Thursday now. I bring the highlighter.

So, was I wrong to do it at her birthday dinner, in front of her whole family?
=== 17 my-bedroom ===
My roommate secretly rented out my bedroom every weekend I was away, so I booked it myself. Am I wrong?

I'm 27, and I've lived with my roommate Owen for almost two years. We found each other through a coworker. He's tidy, quiet, pays his half on time. Honestly, he was the best roommate I'd ever had.

About six months ago, I started staying at my boyfriend Theo's place on weekends. I'd leave Friday after work and come back Monday morning before my shift.

That's when things started feeling off.

The first thing was my sheets. I always tuck the top sheet in at the bottom. One Monday it was tucked in on the sides too, tight, like a hotel.

Then my shampoo was going down way faster than I was using it. Then I found a hair tie on my nightstand. Bright pink. I don't own anything pink.

I asked Owen about it. He laughed and said, "You're probably just tired. You've been running between two apartments."

Then one Monday, there was a little chocolate mint sitting on my pillow. Wrapped in gold foil.

I texted Owen a picture. He said, "Ha, weird. Maybe Theo put it in your bag as a joke."

Theo did not put a mint in my bag.

I started paying attention. My plants were watered when I hadn't watered them. My closet smelled like somebody else's perfume. The little bathroom trash can was always empty on Mondays, and I never empty it.

I told my coworker Dana, and she said, "Girl, he's having somebody over in your room. Probably a girlfriend he doesn't want you to meet."

That actually made sense. Owen had been single for a year. Maybe he was embarrassed. Maybe his place was where they hung out, and they just used my room because it has the bigger bed.

So I decided I would just ask him straight out.

Then Theo sent me a link at work with one line. "Babe. Isn't this your room?"

Would you have confronted Owen that night, or kept quiet and gathered proof? Follow for part 2.
--- part ---
Part 2 of finding out my roommate was secretly renting out my bedroom every weekend I stayed at my boyfriend's.

The link was a short term rental listing. "Sunny private room in artsy apartment. Great for weekend getaways."

The pictures were my room. My bed with my gray comforter. My plants on the windowsill. My framed print of the beach where my grandparents got married. My bookshelf, with my books, in my order.

$89 a night. Fridays and Saturdays only. 4.9 stars. Sixty one reviews.

The host was "Owen." His profile picture was him holding a coffee mug on our balcony.

I read every single review on my lunch break. People loved it. They loved the plants. They loved the mint on the pillow. One woman wrote, "Host mentioned this is his sister's room and she's away a lot. Felt so homey, like staying with family."

His sister. I'm not his sister.

Another one said, "The closet was full of someone's clothes, which was a little weird, but the bed was super comfy."

So strangers had been sleeping in my bed, using my shampoo, and looking through my closet for six months. And Owen was cleaning up after them like a hotel maid every Sunday night before I got home.

I did the math. Two nights a week, $89 a night, about twenty four weekends. That's over $4,000. In my bedroom. While I paid rent on it.

I wanted to scream at him. Theo told me to wait.

He said, "If you confront him now, he deletes the listing and says you're crazy. Get proof first."

So that night, I came home and acted completely normal. Owen was making pasta. He asked how Theo was doing. He offered me some.

I sat at the counter eating his pasta and smiling, thinking about the woman who said my bed was super comfy.

Then I went to my room and pulled out our lease, just to see exactly what I could do about it.

And that's when I found the part that was even worse than the listing.

Would you have told the landlord right away, or waited? Follow for part 3.
--- part ---
Part 3. My roommate was renting out my bedroom on weekends, and then I found out what he did with our lease.

When we moved in, Owen handled the paperwork. He said the landlord was a friend of his uncle's, and he'd sort it all out. He gave me a copy, and I filed it away without really reading it.

Our total rent was printed right on the first page. $1,900 a month.

I've been paying Owen $1,300 every month. He told me my share was more because I have the bigger room with the bathroom.

So he's been paying $600 for his room. I've been paying $1,300 for mine. And on weekends, he's been renting my $1,300 room to strangers for $89 a night and keeping all of it.

I emailed the landlord the next morning. Her name is Mrs. Abernathy, and she's about seventy and very polite. I just asked if she could send me a copy of the lease for my records.

She wrote back within an hour. She said, "Of course, dear. Though I was under the impression you were moving out at the end of this month. Owen said you were relocating for work and that his cousin would be taking your room."

His cousin.

I called her immediately. She had no idea about the rental listing. Subletting is against the lease, and she was not happy. But she also wasn't sure who to believe, because she'd known Owen's family for years and she'd never met me.

That night I finally confronted Owen in the kitchen. I put my phone on the counter with the listing open.

He didn't even look embarrassed. He shrugged and said, "You're never here. The room was just sitting empty. I was going to share the money eventually."

I said, "Eventually? And your cousin moving into my room?"

He said, "You basically live at Theo's anyway. I was doing you a favor."

Then he told me if I made a big deal about it, he'd tell Mrs. Abernathy I was the one who'd been bringing strangers in.

Would you have gone straight to the landlord, or beaten him at his own game? Follow for the final part.
--- part ---
Final part. My roommate rented my bedroom to strangers every weekend, so I booked it myself and brought a guest.

Theo had the idea. If Owen was going to tell Mrs. Abernathy it was me, we needed her to see it with her own eyes.

So Dana made an account and booked my room for that Friday night. Owen accepted in four minutes. He sent her a message that said, "Welcome! My sister is away this weekend, so the room is all yours. Check in is at six. There will be a mint on your pillow."

I told Owen I was staying at Theo's like always. I packed my bag, said goodbye, and left.

At six o'clock, Dana knocked on the door. Owen opened it with a big smile and a set of fresh towels.

Behind Dana was me. And behind me was Mrs. Abernathy, in a purple raincoat, holding her reading glasses.

Owen just froze with the towels in his arms.

Mrs. Abernathy walked straight past him into my room. She looked at the hotel corners on my bed. She looked at the little gold mint on my pillow. Then she turned around and said, "Your cousin, Owen?"

He tried to say it was a one time thing. Dana held up her phone with sixty one reviews on it.

Owen was off the lease by the end of the month. Mrs. Abernathy put it in my name only. And because he'd been overcharging me for two years, she let me deduct what I'd overpaid from my next several months of rent. He had to pay it back to her.

Theo moved in two months later. He takes the bigger room now. Well, we share it.

I washed my sheets four times. I threw out the shampoo. I gave away the comforter.

But I kept the mint. It's in a little dish on my nightstand, still wrapped in gold foil, so I never forget to read the lease.

So, was booking my own bedroom too far, or did he have it coming?
