=== 29 sunday-brunch ===
My girlfriend made me drop my best friend, but she has brunch with her ex every Sunday. Am I wrong?

I'm 31, and my girlfriend Renee is 30. We've been together two years and living together for one. She's a dental hygienist, she's funny, and she remembers everybody's birthday. My mom adores her.

My best friend since college is Hannah. We were roommates junior year. She helped me move four times. She gave the eulogy at my dad's funeral when I couldn't get through mine. There has never been anything between us. Not once. Hannah's been with her wife for six years.

Renee knew all of that from day one. She said it was cute.

Then about eight months into living together, she sat me down on the couch with a glass of wine and a speech she'd clearly practiced.

"I need to feel safe in this relationship," she said. "And I don't feel safe when you text another woman every day."

I said, "Hannah's married. To a woman."

She said, "That doesn't mean she doesn't want you. You're being naive, and honestly, it's kind of disrespectful that you won't even consider how this makes me feel."

Then she cried. Real tears. She said her last boyfriend cheated with his so-called best friend, and she couldn't go through that again.

So I did it. I told Hannah I needed some space. I muted our group chat. I skipped her birthday dinner. Hannah didn't fight me. She just said, "Okay. I'm here if you need me." That was almost worse.

Renee thanked me with a nice dinner. She said, "See? This is what a secure relationship looks like."

Two months later, I noticed the Sundays.

Every Sunday at ten, Renee would leave for what she called brunch with the girls. She'd come back around two with leftovers in a box.

Then one Saturday night, she left her tablet on the counter, logged into her messages. A text popped up from a contact saved as just the letter V.

"Same booth tomorrow? I'll bring the papers again. Please actually look at them this time."

=== 30 vacation-fund ===
My friend held our group vacation fund for two years, then posted from Bali without us. Am I wrong?

I'm 34, and I've had the same four best friends since freshman year of college. Marissa, Joy, Kendra, Lena, and me. We call ourselves the Porch, because we met on the porch of a terrible off campus rental.

Two years ago, for our fifteen year friendship anniversary, we decided to do one big trip. A villa in Costa Rica. Ten days. Private chef. A trip none of us could afford in one shot.

Marissa volunteered to hold the money. She works in insurance billing, she's the organized one, and she said she'd put it in a high yield savings account so it would grow.

So every month, on the first, the four of us sent her $150. Twenty four months. I have every single payment in my Venmo history with the same note: Porch trip, plus a palm tree emoji.

That's $3,600 from me alone. $14,400 from the four of us, plus whatever Marissa put in.

The trip was supposed to be booked this January.

Then last Tuesday, I saw Marissa's story. A beach. Turquoise water. A tray of drinks with little umbrellas. The location tag said Uluwatu, Bali.

There were three other women in the photo. None of them were us.

I texted the group chat. "Marissa, is that Bali? Are you okay?"

She responded eight hours later. "Ha yes! Last minute trip with some girls from spin. Totally separate money, don't worry!!"

I didn't send anything. Because Lena's cousin Dee, who goes to the same spin studio as Marissa, had just texted me a question.

=== 31 her-name ===
My mother-in-law says I'm not real family because I kept my name. She never took my father-in-law's.

I'm 32, and I've been married to Wes for two years. Wes is 34, a high school band director, and the sweetest man alive. When we got married, I kept my last name, Vance. I own a small bakery with my name on the sign, and I didn't want to change my whole life.

Wes didn't care at all. His mom, Diane, cared a lot.

At our wedding, the place cards said Mr. and Mrs. Wesley Pruitt. Diane had them reprinted the morning of. At our first Christmas, she gave me a stocking with Corinne Pruitt sewn on in red glitter. When I gently said my name was still Vance, she patted my hand and said, "For now, sweetheart."

It got worse. At Easter, she told her sister, right in front of me, "She's not really a Pruitt. She's more like a long term guest."

At a cousin's baby shower, she introduced me as Wes's wife, Corinne, she kept her name, with a little face like she was warning everyone I had a rash.

The family has a cabin in the Ozarks. Every Pruitt gets a key and a name on a little wooden sign by the door. When I asked why mine wasn't there, Diane said, "The sign is for real family, Corinne. Real wives take their husband's name. That's what commitment looks like."
