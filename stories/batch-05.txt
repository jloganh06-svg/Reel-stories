My neighbor got my car towed, then I caught her renting out my driveway to strangers. Am I wrong?

I'm 34. I bought my first house two years ago. It's a little blue bungalow three blocks from the college football stadium. I work twelve hour shifts as a lab tech at the hospital, including most Saturdays.

My neighbor Janet is in her sixties. She's lived on our street for thirty years, and she makes sure everyone knows it. She runs the neighborhood group chat, and she has opinions about everyone's trash cans.

Her biggest opinion was about my car.

My driveway is narrow, and my roommate's truck takes it up, so I park on the street. It's a public street. If the spot in front of my house is taken, I park a little in front of Janet's.

She hated it. She left notes on my windshield. "This is not a parking lot." One said, "Entitled people like you are ruining this street."

I brought her cookies and asked if there was a time of day that worked better for her. She took the cookies and said, "Any time you're not here."

Then one Saturday in September, I came out after a double shift, and my car was gone.

Janet had called the city and reported it blocking her mailbox. It was not blocking her mailbox. It was a good six feet away. But it was game day, the tow trucks were out, and nobody measured.

The tow and the ticket cost me $285.

When I went over, she barely opened the door. She said, "Maybe next time you'll think about other people."

The following Saturday, I got off early. I pulled onto my street around noon, and there was a car in my driveway. A minivan I'd never seen in my life. My roommate's truck was parked on the street.

A guy in a team jersey was unloading a cooler. I asked him what he was doing in my driveway.

He held up his phone and said, "I paid for this spot, man. Forty bucks. Talk to the host."

Then he pointed at Janet's house.

Would you have called the tow truck on him, or found out about the host first? Follow for part 2.
--- part ---
Part 2 of my neighbor towing my car for parking near her house, while secretly renting out my driveway.

I didn't call a tow truck. The guy didn't know. He showed me the app.

The listing was titled, "Gameday parking, four minute walk to the stadium." The photo was my driveway. My blue house. My basketball hoop. My recycling bin.

The host's name was Jan P. Her profile picture was a cat. I know that cat. It sits in Janet's front window and glares at me.

The listing had sixty-three reviews. Average rating, 4.9 stars.

I went inside and read every single one.

"Jan was so sweet! She waved us in from the house next door."

"Great spot, host said the owner works Saturdays so we'd never be bothered."

"Jan even let us use the grill in the backyard for a tailgate. Five stars!"

The grill in my backyard. My grill.

I went out back. There was a paper plate stuck under the lid. Somebody had left a bottle opener on my patio table with a sports logo on it.

I did the math from the reviews. She'd been listing my driveway for at least two seasons. Two spots in a narrow driveway, if you squeeze. Forty dollars each. Every home game, plus a few concerts at the stadium in the summer.

She'd made somewhere around three thousand dollars off my driveway. Meanwhile, she'd gotten my car towed for parking six feet from her mailbox and called me entitled.

I asked my roommate Isaac about it. He went quiet. Then he admitted Janet had asked him months ago to park his truck on the street on Saturdays. She'd told him I'd "agreed to it." She'd been giving him $20 a game to keep my driveway clear.

He thought I knew.

So I decided to find out how the booking actually worked. I made a new account in my buddy Darnell's name, and I booked my own driveway for the next home game.

Ten minutes later, the host sent a message.

"Pull all the way up so you're not blocking the street. Owner works Saturdays. He'll never know."

Would you have confronted her before game day, or waited for her to wave you in? Follow for part 3.
--- part ---
Part 3. My neighbor was renting out my driveway and paying my roommate to keep it clear.

That Saturday, I took the day off. I sat on my front porch with a cup of coffee, and at 11:30, Darnell pulled his car into my driveway, right on schedule.

Janet came out of her house waving, like she always does in the reviews. She got halfway across the lawn before she saw me.

She stopped dead.

I said, "Morning, Janet. Darnell says he paid forty bucks for my driveway. Who should he talk to about that?"

For about three seconds, she didn't say anything. Then she switched completely.

She said she'd been "looking after" my property. Keeping strangers from parking there when I wasn't home. She said she'd only charged people "a small fee" to cover her time.

I said, "You let them use my grill."

She said, "Well, it was just sitting there."

Then she pointed at me and said, "You know what? You owe me. I've been keeping your property safe for two years and you've never said thank you once."

When I didn't back down, she flipped again. She raised her voice so the whole street could hear and said I was harassing an elderly woman. A couple of neighbors came out. One of them filmed it.

That night, she posted in the neighborhood group chat. She said a "certain neighbor" had been "aggressive and threatening" and that she no longer felt safe on her own street.

Then she added one more thing. A reminder about the homeowners meeting on Tuesday. She'd proposed a new rule. No overnight street parking in front of other people's homes.

The rule was obviously aimed at me. She wanted to make the thing I did illegal, while she kept making money off my driveway.

A few people liked her post. Somebody commented, "Thank you for always looking out for us, Janet."

I printed the listing. All sixty-three reviews. And the message about the owner who'd never know.

Would you have shown up to that meeting, or let a lawyer handle it? Follow for the final part.
--- part ---
Final part. My neighbor got my car towed while renting out my driveway, so I went to her homeowners meeting.

The meeting was in the community room at the library. About thirty people came, which Isaac told me was a record. I think the video of Janet yelling at me on my own porch had gotten around.

Janet presented her parking rule first. She had a slideshow. One slide said, "Respect your neighbors' curb." She talked for ten minutes about "people who treat this street like a parking lot."

When she asked for questions, I raised my hand.

I said, "I agree with Janet. People shouldn't treat this street like a parking lot. Especially for money."

Then I passed out copies of her listing. Gameday parking, four minute walk. A photo of my blue house. Host, Jan P.

I read three of the reviews out loud. The one about the grill. The one about the owner who works Saturdays. And then I read her own message to Darnell.

"Owner works Saturdays. He'll never know."

The room went quiet. Then an older man in the back started laughing so hard he had to take off his glasses.

Janet said it was a misunderstanding. She said she'd been planning to give me the money. Somebody asked, "Before or after the tow?"

Her parking rule failed, twenty-eight to two. The two were Janet and her sister.

My cousin is a paralegal, and she helped me write a letter. Two weeks later, Janet left an envelope in my mailbox. A check for $3,285. Every game she'd listed, plus my tow, plus the ticket. There was no note.

The app took her listing down after I reported it. She quit running the neighborhood group chat a month later.

I did keep one thing. This season, I listed my own driveway on game days. Forty dollars a spot. I donate it all to the hospital's family fund, and the reviews say the host is very nice.

I also bought a cover for my grill. It has a lock on it.

So, was I wrong to embarrass her in front of the whole neighborhood, or did she have it coming?
